Ouverture
Darkness. Then a single note, rising slowly out of the silence.
The curtain opens on a world between Above and Below. Other rules apply here: whoever descends rarely returns. And whoever returns is no longer the same.
The stage is still empty. But somewhere beyond the light, a river, a gate and a choice are waiting.

Maya
Maya is a warrior from Spain. She has fought battles that others had given up for lost, and she has never hesitated.
Once, she should have. She led Adonis into a fight he did not come back from. He fell at her side, and ever since she has worn that guilt like a second suit of armour.
The living have made their peace with his death. Maya has not. She has heard of a dark lake in Italy where the earth opens and a path leads down into the realm of the dead.
She sets out for it, alone. She means to bring Adonis back.

Stygia
At the end of the path lies the Styx, black and still. No light is mirrored on its water.
The ferrymen see her coming and shake their heads. They do not carry the living across. Out of the mist rise the spirits of the dead, countless, and all of them whisper the same thing: Turn back.
Maya does not turn back. When the spirits bar her way, she draws her weapon. It becomes a fight against shadows, fast and hard, blow after blow to the beat of her own heart.
The dead fall back, one by one. In the end Maya stands alone on the shore. The ferrymen are silent. Then one of them brings the boat alongside.

Dante
On the far shore rises the Gate of Hell. Above the arch, in weathered letters: <i lang="it">Lasciate ogne speranza, voi ch’intrate.</i> Abandon all hope, ye who enter here.
A man is waiting before the gate. His name is Dante, and he has walked this road before – alive, down through every circle and back up again. He knows what waits below.
He warns Maya. He speaks of those who never returned, and of the price the underworld demands for every step. Maya listens, but she does not yield.
Then Dante’s voice turns hard. If she will not listen, he says, he will stop her. By any means.
Maya walks past him through the gate. Dante does not stop her. Not yet.

Catacomb Waltz
Beyond the gate, stairs lead down into endless catacombs. From the depths comes music: a waltz, slow and sweet.
In the vaults the dead are dancing. Couple after couple, they turn in three-four time, round and round, without beginning and without end. Whoever joins the dance, they say, forgets who they were.
Then Maya sees him: Adonis. He is dancing among the dead as if not a day had passed. She steps into the circle and takes his hand, and he leads her as he always led her.
But there is nothing in his eyes. He does not know her.
With every turn it grows easier simply to stay, and Maya’s memories begin to fade. When she tries to break away, the dead hold her fast, and the dance becomes a struggle. At the last moment she tears herself free. She came here to take him with her.

Masquerade
Deeper down, the darkness becomes complete. Then lights flare up, and Maya is standing in the middle of a masked ball.
The spirits wear Venetian masks, white and gold, with empty eyes. They circle Maya, bow, give way, draw closer. Not one of them shows a face.
One of them moves differently. He fights. He seems to foresee every step Maya takes and parries every blow. The spirits draw back and form a ring around the two.
Maya drives him back, step by step. With her final move she tears the mask from his face.
It is Dante. He has kept his word.
For the space of a breath, Maya stands still.

Fracture
A breath is too long.
The spirits have been waiting for exactly this moment. They close the ring, and the masks come from every side. Maya turns – too late.
Something inside her breaks. The beat stumbles, falters, and cannot find its way back. Her weapon falls, then she falls.
Dante fights his way through to her, but by the time he reaches her, she is already still. The spirits withdraw as if nothing had happened. Maya came to bring back one of the dead. Now she is one of them.
Dante kneels beside her. He wanted to stop her. Not like this.

From the Ashes
Down here, everything that dies crumbles to ash. Maya too.
Dante knows how to bring someone back from death. He also knows what it costs: the underworld releases nothing without taking something in return. And he knows he shares the blame for this death.
He offers the only thing he has: his way back. He came down alive and returned alive. That right he now gives away.
From the ashes rises a glow, then a flame, then a breath. Maya opens her eyes. She is alive.
She looks at Dante and does not yet understand what he has done. He does not tell her.

Curtain Fall
Dante leads Maya back, through the hall of masks and up through the catacombs to the gate. They walk in silence. Somewhere behind them, the waltz is still turning.
At the gate, Dante stops. Only now does Maya understand. He can never leave: the way into the light, he gave for her.
Ahead of her lies the road upward. Behind her remain two who are down here because of her: Adonis, who no longer knows her, and Dante, who gave everything for her.
Maya sets off toward the light. One step, then another.
Whether she looks back, no one will ever see. The curtain falls.
